#### Your engineering org got 10x faster and your roadmap did not move. Oji Udezue has a name for why. He calls it the three speed problem. For the past 40 or 50 years, development was the longest pole. Writing quality code takes time. Testing takes time. Making it robust in the cloud, running DevOps, recovering fast when it breaks, all of it takes time. Every process anyone built around software was designed around that constraint. That pole is being cut by roughly 10x. Maybe 20x within five years. But building was never one speed. It is three. Why should we build this. Build it. Get it into people's hands. Collapse only the middle one and you get an equation imbalance, because the two on either side do not run on inference. Figuring out what is worth building is customer bound. You have to talk to people. You have to talk to the market. There is no version where a model shortens the calendar on a discovery call. Getting it into people's hands is customer bound too. Distribution moves at the speed of humans deciding they want the thing. So the constraint did not disappear. It relocated. And this is why every "AI made us 10x faster" claim is doing sleight of hand. It measures the one segment that compresses and quietly assumes the other two came along. They did not. They cannot. The teams that pull ahead over the next two years will be the ones who noticed the bottleneck moved and rebuilt their process around the new one, instead of celebrating a metric on the segment that was never the problem. Speeding up the fast part harder does not fix an imbalance. It deepens it.

#### The scorecard came back with competitive landscape rated strong. That was the reason to stop. This is the part of AI-assisted product work almost nobody has calibrated yet.
