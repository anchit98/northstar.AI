#### Most people treat evals as the thing you build after the model works. A frontier model team inside Meta found out the order runs the other way. It hands you a team that is confident about quality it never actually measured, and the first honest signal arrives from strangers on the internet, after launch. One of the sharpest points in this episode is that a 100% score means you failed. Same idea from the other direction. An eval your model passes has stopped telling you anything. Meta has since said it lost confidence in the people involved and pulled back the entire GenAI org built around that release. A bad PRD gets rewritten. A bad eval costs you the team that built on top of it. And the PMs who can write one are still rarer than the PMs who can talk about one. Own the eval.

#### Olympic pistol shooters wear those sci-fi glasses because the human eye physically cannot do what the sport demands. Yusuf Dikec skipped all of it at age 51, the exact age when eyes need the help most, and shot his way to a silver medal. Here's the problem the gear solves. A pistol shooter has three things at three distances: the rear sight, the front sight, and the target 10 meters away. Your eye can only focus at one distance at a time. Champions are trained to focus on the front sight and let the target blur. The glasses cheat this. A mechanical iris in front of the aiming eye works exactly like the aperture on a camera. Shrink the opening and depth of field expands, so the sight and the target both appear sharp. The blinder covers the other eye so you don't squint, because squinting fatigues the face muscles over a 40-shot match. Dikec spent decades in the Turkish gendarmerie, where you're trained to shoot with both eyes open. A blinder and a lens are built for one-eyed shooting. His entire technique made the equipment useless to him, so he walked out with prescription glasses and yellow drugstore earplugs. And the age detail is the wild part. Past 50, the eye's lens stiffens and near focus collapses. The front sight sits at arm's length, exactly the distance a 51-year-old eye struggles with most. He medaled anyway, in his fifth consecutive Olympics, and became the oldest Olympic medalist in Turkish history. His rival that day wore a lens, a mechanical iris, and a blinder. Dikec beat every shooter in the field except him wearing the same glasses he drives in.

#### Karen Wetterhahn spent twenty years studying what heavy metals do to human cells. A few drops killed her, and she felt nothing for five months. The compound was dimethylmercury. It goes through a disposable latex glove in 15 seconds, possibly instantly, and the safety sheet in her lab said rubber gloves were fine. Nobody had ever run the permeation test. The five months of silence is the part that gets chemists. Dimethylmercury converts slowly into methylmercury, which binds to cysteine in the blood. That complex is almost geometrically identical to methionine, an amino acid your brain imports constantly. The blood-brain barrier runs a transporter called LAT1 whose entire job is recognizing methionine and pulling it inside. It reads the shape and carries the mercury through. #### Psychologists have a name for why people pay extra to eat dinner somewhere a wave can knock over their drink. Benign masochism. #### Spain built a legal system where evicting a squatter took an average of 24 months. Residents in Barcelona figured out barbed wire takes an afternoon. The mechanism behind this is one of the strangest legal inversions in Europe. Article 18 of Spain's constitution makes the home inviolable. Once someone establishes residence, police need a court order to remove them, and the law doesn't ask how the residence was established. Break into an empty apartment, change the locks, start receiving mail, and the property becomes your legally protected dwelling. The person holding the deed now has to sue to enter their own home. A whole market grew in that gap. Firms like Fuera Okupas charge owners €3,000 to send teams of actual MMA fighters to negotiate squatters out, because paying fighters beats waiting two years for a judge. Insurers sell anti-okupa policies. Some owners just hand squatters cash to leave. Spain recorded over 17,000 illegal occupation offenses in 2022, an all-time record, with Catalonia leading the country. The government finally passed a fast-track law in April 2025 promising eviction trials within 15 days. Squatters adapted within a month. Show up with a fabricated rental contract and a utility bill, and your criminal trespass case converts into a civil tenancy dispute the fast-track doesn't cover. Those disputes run through the same backlogged courts that produced the 24-month average. Which brings you back to the balconies. A court order takes two years and can be gamed. Barbed wire works the day you install it. When residents start hardening their own homes, they are pricing in the state's failure to enforce property rights. Razor wire on an apartment balcony is what a 24-month court delay looks like in physical form.
