### Aakash Gupta

#### Tom Holland posts elite CrossFit scores because a 5'8", 145-pound frame is close to the perfect body for the sport's most famous bodyweight test. His 27 rounds of Cindy means 135 pull-ups, 270 push-ups, and 405 air squats. One rep every 1.5 seconds, sustained for 20 straight minutes. CrossFit's own benchmark charts call 11 rounds a beginner score, 19 to 22 advanced, and 24 or more elite. Games-level athletes log around 30. Holland's number sits between elite and professional competitor. The score sounds invented. The math says it's plausible for exactly one body type. His. Cindy punishes mass. Every pull-up moves your full bodyweight, so the workout rewards small, light athletes with enormous relative strength, which describes gymnasts almost perfectly. Holland is one. Before Spider-Man he spent two years playing Billy Elliot in the West End, cast at 12 after training in ballet and acrobatics, doing eight shows a week that closed with a gymnastics routine. Kipping pull-ups, the technique behind every high Cindy score, come straight from gymnastics. Marvel screen-tested thousands of actors for Spider-Man. The one they picked had been training for the part since he was nine.
