#### The ratings board lets every PG-13 movie use the f-word exactly once. #### Apple launched a 0% interest lease this week that makes them more money than charging interest would. The whole mechanism lives in one number. Lease a $2,999 MacBook Pro and you pay $58 a month for 36 months. That's $2,088. The buyout at the end is $911, so the two paths sum to exactly $2,999. No interest, exactly as advertised. Now run Apple's side of each path. If you buy it out, Apple collects $2,999. Same as a cash sale, just spread over three years. If you return it, Apple has collected $2,088 and gets back a 3-year-old MacBook Pro worth $1,500 used, likely more through their own refurb store. Sell it once as a refurb and that single machine grossed Apple roughly $3,600. Around $600 over sticker, on a lease with zero fees. The clever part is where Apple set the residual. Car companies inflate residuals to shrink your monthly payment. Apple deflated theirs. A $911 buyout on a machine worth $1,500 means the exit comes with $600 of built-in equity. Apple left that money on the table on purpose, because decades of leasing data say most people never pick it up. They return the device, feel fine about it, and start a fresh lease on the new model. So every individual customer gets a fair deal on paper, and Apple's blended take across all customers lands comfortably above MSRP. Apple collects its interest in hardware. One returned MacBook at a time.
