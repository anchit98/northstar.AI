#### Most AI PM candidates think the interview is testing whether they know the right answer. I've watched enough of these to know that's not actually what's being scored.Someone asks whether you'd default to an LLM for language, reasoning, and generation, or lean on a hybrid system instead. There's a real answer here. But the answer itself isn't the test. The test is what you do the moment you hit the edge of what you actually know. The candidates who wash out don't wash out because they picked the wrong system. They wash out because they guessed with confidence instead of naming a way to check. "I'd look at recall" or "I'd check F1 on that eval" takes five seconds to say. It sounds smaller than a confident guess. It scores higher every time. Prasad Reddy has watched this play out over and over in interview prep. Bluffing on a technical detail ends interviews faster than actually not knowing the answer does. The instinct to fill silence with confidence is exactly backwards. Silence costs you nothing. A confident wrong guess costs you the room. Name what you'd check.

#### NBA 2K27 costs $149.99 to pre-order. Grand Theft Auto VI, the most anticipated game in entertainment history, costs $99.99. The same company set both prices. Take-Two owns Rockstar and 2K, and once you see the logic, the whole chart snaps into focus. Start with where Take-Two's money actually comes from. Last fiscal year the company booked $6.7 billion, and $5.2 billion of it, 78%, was recurrent consumer spending. Money players spent inside games they already owned. Virtual currency, season passes, card packs. The sale of the game itself is the minority of the business. That makes the $150 Ultra Edition a strange object. Most of what you're buying is a bundle of virtual currency you'll burn through in a few weeks of building your MyPlayer. When it runs out, the in-game store is one tap away. The $150 sticker is less a price than a deposit. EA runs the identical play with FC 27's $149.99 Ultimate Plus Edition, which front-loads the same currency that Ultimate Team converts into billions a year. GTA VI inverts the play. Rockstar can charge almost anything for this game and chose to undercut the annual sports titles by $50. Because the download is customer acquisition. GTA V sold roughly 230 million copies, and then GTA Online generated revenue for 13 straight years, long enough to appear in Take-Two's top earners every single quarter into 2026. The sequel's real product is the online economy that starts after the credits roll. So the pre-order chart people are crying about is internally consistent. The roster update costs $150 because the game is the storefront. The blockbuster costs $100 because the game is the ad. Take-Two priced both, and both prices are optimized for the same customer twelve months from now.

#### An iceberg can flip in under two minutes, and a big one releases about as much energy as the Hiroshima bomb when it does. Here's the physics. Roughly 90% of an iceberg sits below the waterline. The submerged ice melts faster than the exposed ice, so the center of mass slowly creeps upward until the whole structure goes unstable. Past that threshold, millions of tons of ice rotate 90 degrees and years of stored gravitational energy convert to motion in minutes. Physicists at the University of Chicago measured this with scale models. A capsizing iceberg roughly a kilometer thick releases tens of kilotons of TNT equivalent. Towns on Greenland's coast survive these events because of where that energy goes. About 84% dissipates as underwater turbulence, churning the fjord and mixing the water column. The wave that actually reaches shore tops out around 1% of the iceberg's height. That 1% still kills. Greenland posts standing warnings against approaching beaches when large bergs drift close, and settlements have been evacuated over a single iceberg parked offshore. This town is Ilulissat, which literally means "icebergs" in Greenlandic. It sits at the mouth of Sermeq Kujalleq, one of the fastest glaciers on Earth, moving up to 40 meters a day and producing around 10% of all icebergs calved from Greenland. The berg that sank the Titanic most likely started its life in this exact fjord. The people filming from their windows aren't being casual. They know the math. Distance plus that 1% ceiling is the entire safety margin, and it's held for 250 years of the town existing.

#### Sony just patented a PlayStation controller with zero physical buttons. The last company that tried this sold 1.6 million units, then killed the product. The reason it failed is the same reason you can type on a keyboard without looking down. Your thumb finds the X button through proprioception. The raised edge, the travel, the click are landmarks your nervous system maps within hours of picking up a controller. A skilled player executes an input roughly every 100 milliseconds while their eyes never leave the TV. The fingers navigate entirely by feel. Touchscreens won on phones because the display and the input surface are the same piece of glass. Your eyes are already on the thing you're touching, so visual feedback replaces tactile feedback for free. A controller sits ten feet from the screen. Those ten feet are why the button has survived every attempt to kill it. Valve ran this exact experiment. The 2015 Steam Controller replaced face buttons and a stick with haptic trackpads. Court documents from their patent lawsuit revealed 1.6 million sold over four years. Valve discontinued it in 2019 and the next Steam hardware went back to sticks and buttons. So why did Sony file this? Read the patent text. The problem it names is manufacturing, one adaptive surface can fit small hands, large hands, and accessibility needs that currently require separate hardware like the $90 Access Controller. Sony filed 2,256 patents in 2025 alone. This is an option on a future where screens get haptics good enough to fake a click, and until then, your thumbs already know the answer.

#### The Matrix took its name from a 1984 novel written on a manual typewriter by a man who didn't own a computer. 42 years later, that novel finally gets its own screen. William Gibson typed Neuromancer on a 1927 Hermes portable. He coined "cyberspace" before he'd ever logged onto anything. The book swept the Hugo, the Nebula, and the Philip K. Dick award in the same year, the only novel ever to take all three. Then Hollywood spent four decades strip-mining it while calling it unfilmable. The Wachowskis lifted the matrix, the jacked-in hackers, the AI pulling strings behind a corporate veil. Every razor-girl assassin in sci-fi traces back to Molly. Johnny Mnemonic, an actual Gibson adaptation, flopped so hard in 1995 that studios treated his work as radioactive for a generation. At least five directors attached and detached over the years. Chris Cunningham in 2000. Vincenzo Natali in 2010. Tim Miller at Fox in 2017. Each attempt died the same death. By the time the technology existed to film Neuromancer, audiences had watched its ideas in a dozen movies that borrowed them first. The original became unfilmable because it looked like a copy of its own copies. Now the timing loops back on itself. A story about a rogue AI maneuvering to escape its corporate constraints, written before the web existed, lands in January 2027 as the least speculative thing on television. And one detail makes the whole arc perfect. When Neuromancer royalties came in, Gibson bought his first computer, an Apple IIc. Four decades later, Apple is the company putting his book on screen.

#### Every AI PM interview at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and Nvidia eventually asks the same question. Most candidates get it wrong before they finish their sentence. Why does the model hallucinate. The instinct is to treat it as a bug. Something a longer prompt or a sterner instruction fixes. That instinct is what filters candidates out in the first ten seconds. An LLM never knows anything. It scores the next token across a probability distribution and picks from the top, one at a time. Ask for the capital of France and Paris wins because that pairing shows up constantly in training data, not because the model looked it up. Guess wrong with the same confidence it guesses right and you get a hallucination. Same mechanism that produces every correct answer. This time it just lands on the wrong token. No amount of prompting changes that math. Grounding does. Feed the model real documents, or a tool that fetches live data, then check whether the answer traces back to what it retrieved. Prasad Reddy ran candidates through this exact distinction in a live mock interview, the same format top AI PM candidates get graded on. The ones who pass aren't promising better prompts will fix hallucination. They're reaching for grounding first. Stop prompting harder.

#### There's a specific way to answer "why do LLMs hallucinate" in an interview, and most candidates only get halfway there before running out of anything useful to say. The strong version starts by reframing the premise. The model is doing exactly what it's built to do, predicting the next most likely token, and nothing in that process forces the prediction back to anything real. Confidence and correctness were never linked to begin with. A longer, more carefully worded prompt does almost nothing to fix this. The actual fix is grounding, retrieval that pulls in real content or a tool call that fetches real data, so the model has something outside its own guess to answer from. That's the answer most candidates who've done their homework can give. Here's where it splits. The stronger answer doesn't stop at grounding as a concept. It measures whether grounding is actually working, and treats that as two separate, independently testable failures. Faithfulness: does the final answer trace back to what got retrieved? Retrieval quality: did the system pull the right snippet in the first place? Confusing those two is how teams spend weeks fixing the wrong problem. I gave this exact answer in a mock interview segment, including the part most people would edit out: admitting I'd confirm the precise metric with my team before locking it in. Naming the uncertainty instead of papering over it reads as more senior than pretending to already know. Name both failures.
